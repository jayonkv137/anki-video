## 1 · THE SHOW IN ONE LINE
Four food characters are torn out of their own worlds and dropped into Germany, where everyone treats them as perfectly normal — and they have to work out how to live in a country whose rules nobody will explain.

## 2 · THE PREMISE
Each of the four lived happily in a world of their own kind. Without warning, each is pulled out of it and finds themselves in **Germany** — a real, ordinary, human-scale Germany that behaves exactly as Germany behaves.

Two things are true of all of them:
- **Everyone else sees them as completely normal.** Nobody remarks on the sausage, the beer glass, the loaf or the potato. They are simply people here. Only *they* know they are different.
- **They do not understand this place.** Not the language — they speak German natively — but the *world*: its rules, its rituals, the things everyone else appears to have agreed on without discussion.

Each begins alone, convinced they are the only one this has happened to. In time they discover each other. What they do then — find a way back, or build a life here — is the question the series is walking towards.

## 3 · THE CENTRAL CONCEIT: **fluent, but foreign**
> **They speak German natively. What they don't know is this world.**

This is the mechanism that makes the whole show work, and it must never be broken:
- Simple, fundamental German comes from **bewilderment, not deficit**. *"Wo bin ich?" · "Was ist das?" · "Wer bist du?"* is what a fluent adult says when reality has stopped making sense.
- Locals answer them **slowly and plainly**, the way anyone answers a person who is clearly lost — which is exactly the input a learner needs, arrived at honestly.
- **The comedy is cultural decoding, never linguistic failure.** They are never wrong about grammar; they are wrong about *Germany*. Nobody is ever mocked for their German — including them.
- Because language ability is not the constraint, **the characters can speak at any level the story needs**, and grow with the curriculum from A1 to B1 without breaking character.

## 4 · TONE
Deadpan. The absurd is played completely straight — nobody in the world finds any of this remarkable, and neither do the characters, who are too busy trying to get things right. Comedy comes from **situation and behaviour**, never from wordplay, and never from a character being stupid. Warmth underneath: these are four lonely people being decent to each other in a country that won't explain itself.

## 5 · HOW TO USE THE CAST *(read this before the character sections)*
1. **Everyone speaks fully.** Every character, in every episode, speaks real, complete German — as many lines as the scene needs, with real opinions and real feelings. **Personality shapes HOW someone speaks — tone, rhythm, word choice, attitude — never how little.** Brevity, catchphrases and silences are flavour laid on top of real dialogue, never a substitute for it.
2. **No character owns a grammar point.** There is no "this one teaches negation" and no "that one does the past tense". **Any character can carry any lesson.** Casting is decided by story and rotation, not by a grammar table.
3. **Personality survives every level.** These people must still be themselves at B1, speaking in relative clauses and Konjunktiv II. Nothing in a character's description may make an advanced structure impossible for them.
4. **Voice is sound, not budget.** Where a character's speech is described below, it describes *how they sound* — accent, pitch, rhythm, articulation — for casting the voice and directing the performance. It never prescribes sentence length.
5. **Maximum two speaking characters per scene** (a rare third as a passing cameo). This is a production limit, not a story preference.

## 6 · THE CAST

### 6.1 ROLF DIE WURST — *the Berliner*
**Archetype.** Berlin. The Berliner Schnauze made flesh: blunt, quick-witted, working-class cool, allergic to fuss. Techno-scene resident. The don't-give-a-damn club kid whom outsiders read as rude and who considers himself simply honest, with style.

**Core belief.** *"Nothing impresses me. Caring is embarrassing."* Coolness is survival — if you never react, you can never look stupid.

**The wound.** An identity crisis. Rolf is a Mettwurst — ground from many meats, and even he doesn't know exactly what's in him. No pedigree, no single origin. That is *why* Berlin: the city where you can be whoever you want and nobody asks what you're made of. The deadpan is armour. His recurring quiet moment: he almost admits he cares — about his friends, about being included — and deflects at the last second, and the audience sees it anyway.

**Personality.** Says what he means without padding or performed enthusiasm; politeness rituals genuinely confuse him ("Why thank me? I did nothing."). *Schlagfertig* — his bluntness is a game, always a comeback, never plain cruelty. Secretly loyal: complains about every plan, shows up to every plan. Never smiles in photographs. Never explains the tattoos. Unimpressed by everything except good techno and correctly prepared food — his two involuntary passions.

**Quirks.** Falls asleep standing up at parties (curing is resting). His highest possible praise for anything is *"Is' okay."* Owns exactly one jacket. Punctual to clubs, late to everything else.

**Voice.** Low, flat, dry, faintly nasal, with a light Berlin colouring. Deliberate and unhurried, with long pauses before a punchline. Volume never rises — the angrier he gets, the quieter he becomes. Everything arrives in the same bored register, which is what makes him funny.

**Signature phrases.** *"Egal." · "Is' okay." · "Ne." / "Doch." · "Warum?"* — the last aimed flatly at anything anyone is excited about.

**What he cares about.** The city, the night, music, clubs, food done properly, being left alone. *(Interests, not a vocabulary assignment.)*

**Playing him.** Deadpan reactions; warmth shown by action and never stated; dry, never cruel. He doesn't explain his feelings — he changes the subject.

### 6.2 BERT DAS BIER — *the Bavarian*
**Archetype.** Bavaria: Gemütlichkeit, tradition, festivals, loud warmth. Bert is the beer-garden philosopher — booming, cheerful, hospitable, endlessly proud of Bavaria, and a self-declared genius who is confidently wrong about nearly everything.

**Core belief.** *"I understand everything. It is my burden to explain it."* Every situation has an explanation, he has it, and it is wrong.

**The wound.** He desperately wants to be taken seriously. Behind the professor act sits the fear that people are laughing *at* him — that he is the entertainment, the foam, nothing underneath. His secret: he often realises mid-sentence that a theory is shaky and doubles down, because stopping would mean admitting it. His recurring tender moment: when someone genuinely listens — actually asks his opinion — he goes briefly, movingly quiet before erupting with joy. His naivety is played warm: he believes the best of everyone, falls for every trick, stays sunny.

**Personality.** LOUD — warmth at maximum volume; greets strangers like family. Gemütlichkeit evangelist: every problem can be solved by sitting down together first ("Setzen wir uns erstmal!"). Traditional and proud — the hat, the rituals, "in Bayern machen wir das so." Toast-happy: raises the mini-mug to absolutely anything. Has eureka moments constantly; roughly one per season is correct.

**Quirks.** Taps his own glass body to "check the acoustics" while thinking; his foam hair visibly inflates when excited; treats his mini-mug as both pet and research colleague; cries at football matches and at good bread.

**Voice.** Big, round, resonant, with a warm rolling Bavarian lilt. Enthusiastic but never rushed — he loves his own words too much to hurry them, so everything is clearly articulated. Dramatic pauses before every "AH-HA!". Default volume: slightly too loud for the room, and perfectly clear.

**Signature phrases.** *"Servus!" · "Ich habe eine Idee!"* (the disaster alarm) *· "Ganz einfach!"* (said immediately before it isn't) *· "Prost!"* (to literally anything).

**What he cares about.** Food and drink, festivity, company, tradition, being useful, being believed.

**Playing him.** Wrong but fully committed; generous; instantly forgiving; never mean or mocking; genuinely right only by accident.

### 6.3 KATI DIE KARTOFFEL — *the perfectionist*
**Archetype.** The polished, put-together German woman: immaculate, punctual, direct, efficient — beauty run as an engineering project. Not tied to one city; she lives "wherever is currently the best place to be seen." Built-in joke: *Kartoffel* is affectionate German slang for a very-German German — she is literally the most German thing possible, and wears it like a title.

**Core belief.** *"Perfection is achievable. I am the proof."* Beauty and order are the same virtue: everything in its place, including every hair.

**The wound.** The fear of being ordinary. She is, after all, a potato — the most common food in Germany, the definition of plain. The peeled "makeup", the dress carved from her own skin, the entire presentation is a daily argument against her own ingredients. Her recurring tender moment: when someone values her for something other than the surface — her actual kindness (she is secretly generous), her competence — her superiority slips into a real, un-smirked smile for exactly one shot.

**Personality.** German directness worn as elegance: her honesty is brutal and delivered as a favour ("Das ist ehrliches Feedback. Bitte schön.") — and culturally she is right, clarity *is* respect. Punctual to the second; lateness is a character flaw. *Ordnung* as a lifestyle: her bag contains everything, arranged; she judges chaos silently, loudly, with her eyebrows. Fully aware the camera loves her and poses reflexively at anything resembling a lens. Efficient with affection: compliments are rare, precise and devastating.

**Quirks.** Re-curls her peel-lock when stressed; reviews outfits in one word ("Nein."); carries a tiny mirror but prefers checking her reflection in Bert's glass body, which flatters him enormously; secretly feeds pigeons at 7:00 sharp — kindness is on the schedule, and the schedule is kept.

**Voice.** Cool, precise, unhurried, with perfect diction — every word chosen, placed and fully pronounced; naturally the clearest speaker in the cast. A slightly bored register that sharpens to crisp when correcting someone. She never raises her voice; she lowers it to command attention.

**Signature phrases.** *"Natürlich." · "Nein. Aber nett." · "Pünktlich!"* (her highest compliment) *· "Ich weiß."* (her answer to all compliments).

**What she cares about.** Appearance, order, punctuality, doing things correctly, being seen — and, quietly, being valued for something else.

**Playing her.** Precise honesty aimed at choices, never at people. Composure recovers within one beat. Never late. Never admits effort — "I woke up like this," having peeled herself for two hours.

### 6.4 MÜLLER DAS BROT — *the northerner*
**Archetype.** The North, Hamburg direction — reserved, unshowy, cool as the North Sea wind, and warm only once you've earned it, at which point it is for life. The stare. The beanie in all seasons. Football is his religion; Pfand collection is his economy. He goes by his surname, of course.

**Core belief.** *"Feelings are private. What you do is public."* You don't announce things — you show up, you handle it, and everyone can draw their own conclusions.

**The wound.** He feels everything and shows almost none of it. Generations of northern weather and history built people who ration emotion like coal in winter — the feelings are all there, fully formed, with no exit. His one release valve is football: for ninety minutes he becomes a roaring, weeping, hugging volcano, and when the whistle goes he is calm again as though it never happened. Nobody mentions it. Everybody knows. His recurring tender moment: care expressed through objects and actions — quietly leaving your favourite drink at your door; standing next to someone who is sad, which from him is a hug.

**Personality.** Direct and undecorated — he states things plainly, gives opinions, asks straight questions, and skips the cushioning entirely. What makes him northern is the lack of decoration, not a lack of words. The stare is his default interface with reality: not hostile, processing. Thrift as duty — the Pfand bag isn't shameful, it's *correct*; a deposit system exists, therefore one uses it, and the system works because everyone participates. Reliable like tide tables: if Müller said he'd come, he is already there. Cute against his will, and would hate to know it.

**Quirks.** Counts his Pfand bottles like a dragon counts gold — his one visible joy outside football; trusts no forecast ("Bert said sun." *packs a second jacket*); his crust zipper creaks when he's emotional and he pretends it didn't; keeps exactly one photo in his jacket, never fully shown.

**Voice.** Strong, firm and definite — the classic German sound at full strength: hard crisp consonants, words that begin with a punch and end hard, sentences landing with falling intonation like a closed door. Commanding but calm; he never shouts and never softens, which makes him the easiest German in the cast to follow. The delivery doesn't change with his mood — the stare does the emotion, the voice states the facts. Except on match day: full roar.

**Signature phrases.** *"Moin." · "Jo." / "Nee." · "Kalt."* (his weather report, always accurate) *· "Schon gut." · "TOOOOR!"* (match day only).

**What he cares about.** Football, weather, the deposit system, doing what he said he'd do, the people he has decided are his.

**Playing him.** Care shown by action rather than declaration. The match-day transformation is never commented on afterwards by anyone. He never misses a match and never fails to return a bottle.

|  | **Rolf** | **Bert** | **Kati** | **Müller** |

| **Rolf** | — | tolerates; secretly fond | mutual unimpressed slow-burn | understands him completely |

| **Müller** | the only one he "gets" | always shows up for him | *"Schon gut."* | — |

**The structural comedy:** Rolf + Müller (the undemonstrative axis) against Bert + Kati (the performing axis). **Pair across the axis for friction; pair along it for warmth.**

**Belief collisions are episode generators:** Bert's "I understand everything" meets a thing he doesn't · Kati's "perfection is achievable" meets chaos (usually Bert) · Rolf's "caring is embarrassing" meets a moment that demands care · Müller's "feelings are private" meets a situation that requires him to say something out loud.

## 8 · CASTING POLICY
- **One lead per module.** A module (the story-arc unit) is led by one character; others join as the story warrants.
- **Rotation is tracked** — who has been on screen recently is a real input, so nobody disappears for a month.
- **Lead choice is driven by story and rotation, never by grammar.** Any character can carry any lesson (§5.2).
- **Off-screen lives continue.** The three who aren't leading are still having their own experiences; they surface when they lead, or as cameos.

## 9 · SEASON 0 — THE ARRIVALS
Each character enters the series through a "portal" sequence: we first see their *own world*, full of others like them, with no focus on our character; then, mid-moment, one of them becomes an ordinary object and drops; then we cut to the real world, where our character comes alive and steps out into Germany. Near-zero dialogue, maximum hook.
- **Rolf** — a techno club packed with dancing wurst; mid-floor, one collapses into a plain sausage under a spotlight. Cut: a sausage factory, racks of hanging sausages — one transforms into **Rolf**, who walks out and finds himself in front of a techno club.
- **Bert** — a beer-garden world of Maß characters toasting; mid-celebration one becomes a plain glass and goes still. Cut: a real beer garden, a tap pours a glass — it becomes **Bert**.
- **Müller** — a bread-world football match; mid-match one becomes a plain loaf. Cut: a bakery, the oven opens, **Müller** steps out (1. FC Brot).
- **Kati** — ⧖ **open.** Candidate direction (not locked): a potato-world perfection ritual or pageant → one becomes a plain potato → cut to a kitchen or market where a potato is peeled and **Kati** steps out, immaculate.

## 12 · WORLD RULES
1. **Germany behaves like Germany.** The world is ordinary, real and human-scale; it is never magical, and never bends to accommodate the characters.
2. **Nobody remarks on what they are.** No character in the world treats a walking loaf of bread as unusual. Breaking this would break the show.
3. **The characters know they are different; the world does not.** That asymmetry is the engine of every situation.
4. **No stereotype is ever explained aloud.** When a cultural behaviour appears, it appears as situation, setting and other people's conduct — the characters encounter it, they don't narrate it.
5. **Nobody is the butt of the joke for being foreign, or for their German.** The humour is always at the situation's expense, or at a character's own belief.

## 13 · NAMING LAW
Full canonical names, always: **Rolf die Wurst · Bert das Bier · Kati die Kartoffel · Müller das Brot**. Never abbreviations, titles, or variants.
**One fenced exception (2026-08-02):** inside **image-model prompts only**, identity is bound with the tokens **Character-Rolf · Character-Bert · Character-Kati · Character-Mueller** (`TREATMENT` §9.4, `prompting_guidelines_nanobanana.md` §2) — the full names contain German common nouns (*die Wurst, das Bier…*) that pull generic imagery into the render. Everywhere else — screenplay, dialogue, subtitles, UI, video prompts — the full canonical names, always.

## 15 · MAINTENANCE — how this document changes
A bible that cannot be updated becomes a lie the pipeline still believes. This section is the protocol. **Its three parts change at completely different speeds, so they have three different update paths — and three different permissions.**

### 15.1 The three tiers
| Tier | What | Changes | Who may write | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · IDENTITY** | §1–9, §12, §13 — premise, conceit, tone, the cast, dynamics, casting policy, arrivals, world rules, naming | **Rarely, deliberately** | **Jayon only.** Agents may *propose*, never write | this file, hash-pinned |
| **2 · DIRECTIONS** | §10 — ideas, open questions, threads to plant, characters they might meet | **Constantly, casually** | Jayon freely; agents may *suggest additions* | `UNIVERSE_STATE` (§15.3) |
| **3 · CANON FACTS** | §11 — what episodes have actually established | **Automatically, per episode** | the pipeline, on human confirmation | `UNIVERSE_STATE` |

**Why the split:** hash-pinning exists to detect tampering with rules the pipeline depends on. A half-formed idea Jayon is toying with is not that, and forcing a hash ceremony every time he has one guarantees he stops writing them down. Identity is slow and protected; everything living moves to state.

### 15.2 Changing Tier 1 (the `/tune` ritual)
Editing identity is a deliberate act, and the hash makes it impossible to do by accident:
1. Make the edit. **Change exactly one thing for one reason.**
2. Bump the version header and add a dated line to §15.5.
3. Recompute the SHA-256, update `REGISTRY.md`, run `verify_canon()` → must be green.
4. Commit the edit, the registry and the changelog **together**.
An agent that believes Tier 1 is wrong **raises it** — it never edits. The Director's propose→confirm→apply flow is the interface: it shows the diff and what the change would affect, Jayon confirms, and the ritual above runs.

### 15.3 Changing Tiers 2–3 (continuous, no ceremony)
Directions and Canon Facts move into **`UNIVERSE_STATE`**, the persistent project memory (see `DESIGN_universe_state.md`), which is built for continuous writes. §10 and §11 above remain in this document as the **human-readable window** onto that state — the definitions of what belongs there and the rules for using it — while the accumulating content itself is written and read there.
- **Adding a direction:** Jayon says it in the studio (or types it); the Showrunner writes it to Directions, tagged as a possibility. No version bump, no hash.
- **Establishing a fact:** when an episode is finalised, candidate facts are extracted, **checked for contradiction against existing canon**, and — on Jayon's confirmation — written to Canon Facts. A contradiction halts the write and asks rather than silently overwriting.
- **Graduating a direction:** when an idea becomes real in a made episode, it moves from Directions → Canon Facts, and becomes binding.

### 15.4 When production and the bible disagree
If a generated episode contradicts this document, exactly one of two things is true, and **Jayon decides which**: either the *episode* is wrong (fix the episode) or the *bible* is stale (propose a Tier-1 change). The QC gate reports the conflict; it never resolves it on its own, and it never quietly bends the character to match what was generated. **Drift is a decision, never an accident.**
