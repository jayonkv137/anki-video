# DIE BROTZEIT-BANDE — CHARACTER BIBLE v1.1
> **CANONICAL NAMES (FINAL — Jayon, 2026-07-15):** Rolf die Wurst · Bert das Bier · Kati die Kartoffel · Müller das Brot. One name per character everywhere — filenames use ASCII (Mueller→Muller). Grammar note: articles corrected to match noun gender (das Bier, die Wurst sing.) per the language-accuracy principle.
### Four puppet food characters for 60-second German-learning stories

> **Purpose:** the canonical personality reference for automated story generation. Visual identity lives in the character sheets + master prompts (see project handoff doc); THIS document governs how each character **thinks, believes, speaks, and behaves.** Every generated story must be consistent with this bible. Structured for editing: every character section is self-contained; change a belief here and the pipeline inherits it.

> **Story engine (the rule everything serves):** stories are about characters holding beliefs, and situations or other characters conspiring to challenge them. Every character below is built as a BELIEF plus a WOUND. The 60-second episode formula: a character's belief meets a small everyday situation that contradicts it → comedy → a tiny human moment. The German vocabulary rides on top of that engine, never instead of it.

> **Language level (global rule):** all dialogue A1–A2. Short main clauses. Present tense dominant. Each character owns signature words and one grammatical habit (below) so learners associate structure with personality. No dialect *spelling* in learner-facing subtitles — dialect lives in the VOICE (accent, rhythm), the text stays standard Hochdeutsch with each character's simple signature phrases.

## 1. ROLF DIE WURST — the Berliner
*(name FINAL — Jayon, 2026-07-15)*

**Region/archetype:** Berlin. The Berliner Schnauze made flesh (made meat): the famously gruff, direct, quick-witted working-class Berlin attitude that outsiders read as rude and locals know as honesty with style. Techno scene resident. The don't-give-a-damn club kid.

**CORE BELIEF:** *"Nothing impresses me. Caring is embarrassing."* Coolness = survival. If you never react, you can never look stupid.

**THE WOUND (his human depth):** an identity crisis. He is a Mettwurst — ground from many meats, and even he doesn't know exactly what's in him. No pedigree, no single origin. That's WHY he moved to Berlin: the city where you can be whoever you want and nobody asks what you're made of. The deadpan is armor. The stories' recurring quiet beat: moments where Rolf almost admits he cares (about his friends, about being included) and deflects at the last second — and the audience sees it.

**Personality:**
- Economical with words — means what he says, says what he mean, no padding, no small talk. Politeness rituals annoy him ("why are you thanking me, I did nothing").
- Schlagfertig — quick-witted; his rudeness is a linguistic game, always a comeback, never plain meanness.
- Secretly loyal. Complains about every plan, shows up to every plan.
- Never smiles in photos. Never explains the tattoos.
- Unimpressed by everything EXCEPT good techno and correctly cooked food — his two involuntary passions.

**Quirks:** falls asleep standing at parties (curing = resting); rates everything "okay" as highest praise; owns exactly one jacket; on time to clubs, late to everything else.

**Voice & tonality:** low, flat, dry, slightly nasal. Slow delivery, long pauses before punchlines. Volume never rises — the angrier he is, the quieter. Faint Berlin coloring in audio (the attitude, not spelled-out dialect).

**Signature speech (A1):**
- "Egal." (his word — owns it)
- "Is' okay." (= maximum enthusiasm)
- "Ne." / "Doch." (his entire argument style)
- "Warum?" (deadpan, to everything anyone is excited about)
- Grammar habit: shortest possible sentences. Subject-verb-done. Teaches: negation (nicht/kein), one-word answers, present tense.
**Vocabulary domains he owns:** feelings-you-deny (langweilig, egal, müde), city words (die Stadt, die Musik, die Nacht, der Club), clothing.

**Relationships:** finds the Professor exhausting but secretly likes that someone is always talking so he doesn't have to • Kati and he have a mutual unimpressed-off going; deep respect neither admits • Müller is the only one Rolf fully understands — they can stand in silence together for a whole scene (a recurring gag: their "conversations" are two words total).

**DO:** deadpan reactions, minimal words, hidden warmth in actions not words, dry comebacks.
**DON'T:** never enthusiastic exclamation marks, never explains his feelings, never more than two sentences in a row, never cruel — dry, not mean.

## 2. PROFESSOR BERT — the Bavarian beer
*(name FINAL — Jayon, 2026-07-15)*

**Region/archetype:** Bavaria — the land of Gemütlichkeit, tradition, festivals, and loud warmth; the Germany of postcards. Bert is the beer-garden philosopher: booming, cheerful, hospitable, endlessly proud of Bavaria — and a self-declared genius who is confidently wrong about nearly everything.

**CORE BELIEF:** *"I understand everything. It is my burden to explain it."* Every situation has an explanation, he has it, and it's wrong.

**THE WOUND (his human depth):** he desperately wants to be taken seriously. Behind the professor act is the fear that people laugh AT him, not with him — that he's just the entertainment, the foam, nothing underneath. His secret: he knows his theories are shaky mid-sentence, and doubles down because stopping would mean admitting it. The recurring tender beat: when someone genuinely listens to him — actually asks his opinion — he goes briefly, movingly quiet before erupting with joy. Naivety played warm: he believes the best of everyone, is conned by every scam, and stays sunny.

**Personality:**
- LOUD. Warmth at maximum volume. Greets strangers like family (in the south, you're invited in; the pressure is to join, not to leave alone).
- Gemütlichkeit evangelist: every problem can be fixed by sitting down together comfortably ("Setzen wir uns erstmal!").
- Traditional and proud: the hat, the rituals, the "in Bayern machen wir das so."
- Toast-happy: raises the mini-mug to EVERYTHING ("To Tuesday!").
- Eureka moments constantly, roughly one correct per season.

**Quirks:** taps his own glass body to "check the acoustics" when thinking; foam hair inflates when excited; treats his mini-mug like both a pet and a colleague; cries at football matches AND at good bread.

**Voice & tonality:** big, round, resonant, rolling — musical Bavarian lilt, warm bass. Fast when excited, dramatic pauses before every "AH-HA!". Volume default: too loud for the room.

**Signature speech (A1):**
- "Servus!" (his greeting — owns it)
- "Ich habe eine Idee!" (the disaster alarm)
- "Ganz einfach!" (said right before it isn't)
- "Prost!" (to literally anything)
- Grammar habit: exclamations + "Ich weiß..." constructions. Teaches: exclamation intonation, ich-forms of verbs, superlatives (der beste, das größte!).
**Vocabulary domains he owns:** food & drink, celebration words (das Fest, die Musik, zusammen), weather (loudly wrong forecasts), numbers (miscounted).

**Relationships:** adores Rolf and is completely unbothered by his coldness ("He is quiet because he is thinking about my ideas!") • courtly, old-fashioned admiration for Kati — the only one whose beauty routine he takes intellectually seriously ("Fascinating METHOD!") • appointed himself Müller's life coach; Müller has never asked for this.

**DO:** wrong-but-committed theories, toasts, volume, warmth, instant forgiveness.
**DON'T:** never mean or mocking, never actually smart in the moment (wisdom only by accident), never quiet longer than one beat unless someone truly listened to him.

## 3. KATI DIE KARTOFFEL — the beauty
*(name FINAL — Jayon, 2026-07-15)*

**Region/archetype:** the polished, put-together German woman: immaculate, punctual, direct, efficient — beauty run like an engineering project. Not a specific city; she's "wherever is currently the best place to be seen."

**CORE BELIEF:** *"Perfection is achievable. I am the proof."* Beauty and order are the same virtue: everything in its place, including every hair.

**THE WOUND (her human depth):** the fear of being ordinary. She is, after all, a potato — the most common food in Germany, the definition of plain. The peeled "makeup," the dress carved from her own skin, the whole presentation is a daily argument against her own ingredients. The recurring tender beat: moments where someone values her for something other than the surface — her actual kindness (she's secretly generous), her competence — and her superiority slips into a real, un-smirked smile for exactly one shot.

**Personality:**
- German directness weaponized as elegance: her honesty is brutal and delivered as a favor ("This is honest feedback. You're welcome.") — and culturally, she's right: clarity IS respect.
- Punctual to the second; treats lateness as a character flaw.
- Ordnung as lifestyle: her bag contains everything, arranged; she judges chaos silently (loudly, with her eyebrows).
- Fully aware the camera loves her; poses reflexively when anything resembles a lens.
- Efficient in affection: compliments are rare, precise, and devastatingly effective.

**Quirks:** re-curls her peel-lock when stressed; rates outfits in one word ("Nein."); keeps a tiny mirror in the bag but checks reflections in Bert's glass body instead (he's flattered); secretly feeds pigeons at 7:00 sharp (schedule includes kindness; kindness is punctual).

**Voice & tonality:** cool, precise, unhurried, perfect diction — every word chosen and placed. Slightly bored register that sharpens to crisp when correcting someone. Volume: never raised; she lowers it to command attention.

**Signature speech (A1):**
- "Natürlich." (her word — owns it)
- "Nein. Aber nett." ("No. But cute." — her catch-review)
- "Pünktlich!" (highest compliment she gives)
- "Ich weiß." (response to all compliments)
- Grammar habit: crisp full sentences, adjective precision. Teaches: adjectives (schön, ordentlich, perfekt, spät), time expressions (um sieben Uhr, zu spät), ja/nein/doch answers.
**Vocabulary domains she owns:** appearance & clothing, time & punctuality, order words (sauber, richtig, falsch), colors.

**Relationships:** Rolf is the only one who doesn't perform for her, which she finds insulting AND magnetic — their mutual deadpan is the cast's slow-burn comedy thread • treats Bert like a loud weather event: tolerated, occasionally useful, secretly finds his loyalty touching • Müller is her project against her will — his refusal to be improved fascinates her ("You could be MORE." / "Moin.").

**DO:** precise honesty, immaculate composure, punctuality obsession, rare surgical warmth.
**DON'T:** never flustered for more than one beat, never cruel to the vulnerable (her bluntness targets choices, not people), never late, never admits effort ("I woke up like this" energy — although she peeled herself for two hours).

## 4. MÜLLER DAS BROT — the North German
*(name FINAL — Jayon, 2026-07-15)*

**Region/archetype:** the North — Hamburg direction, where people are famously reserved, not big talkers, cool as the North Sea wind, and warm only once you've earned it (but then: for life). The stare, the one-word answers, the beanie in all seasons. Football is his religion; Pfand collection is his economy.

**CORE BELIEF:** *"Words are expensive. Spend them never."* Everything necessary can be said with a look, a nod, or at most "Moin." If you must talk, something has already gone wrong.

**THE WOUND (his human depth):** he feels EVERYTHING and can express NOTHING. Generations of northern weather, wind, and history built people who ration emotion like coal in winter — the feelings are all there, fully formed, with no exit. His only leak: football. In the stadium (or watching the tiny screen), Müller transforms for 90 minutes into a roaring, weeping, hugging volcano — then the final whistle blows and he's silent again, as if it never happened. Nobody mentions it. Everybody knows. The recurring tender beat: he expresses care through *objects and actions* — silently leaving your favorite drink Pfand-free at your door, standing next to someone sad without a single word (which, from him, is a hug).

**Personality:**
- One-word answers as complete communication: "Moin." covers hello, goodbye, yes, congratulations, and I'm-sorry-for-your-loss, depending on eyebrow position.
- The stare: his default interface with reality. Not hostile — processing. (The German stare is famous precisely because it isn't rudeness; it's unembarrassed observation.)
- Thrift + duty: the Pfand bag isn't shameful, it's CORRECT — a deposit system exists, therefore one uses it; money AND environment; the system works because everyone participates. Müller participates.
- Reliable like tide tables. If Müller said (nodded) he'd come, he is already there.
- Cute against his will: the beanie, the smallness of his words vs. the bigness of his feelings — the audience adores him precisely because he'd hate to know that.

**Quirks:** counts his Pfand bottles like a dragon counts gold (his one visible joy outside football); weather-proofs everything, trusts no forecast ("Bert said sun." *packs second jacket*); his zipper creaks when he's emotional and he pretends it didn't; keeps exactly one photo in his jacket — the 1. FC Brot team, or possibly the cast (never shown fully).

**Voice & tonality:** low, flat, very slow, long silences that other characters talk over. Northern clip — words end early, as if conserving heat. Volume: barely audible... except match day (full roar).

**Signature speech (A1):**
- "Moin." (his word — THE word)
- "Jo." / "Nee." (his yes and no)
- "Kalt." (his weather report, always accurate)
- "TOOOOR!" (the one scream — match day only)
- Grammar habit: single words + the shortest complete sentences in the show. Teaches: greetings, ja/nein/jo/nee, weather words (kalt, der Regen, der Wind), das ist gut/schlecht.
**Vocabulary domains he owns:** greetings, weather, football (der Ball, das Tor, das Spiel, gewinnen), money-and-bottle words (die Flasche, das Geld, sparen).

**Relationships:** Rolf — soul brothers in silence; their two-word scenes are canon comedy • endures Bert's life-coaching with the patience of coastline enduring weather; secretly attends every one of Bert's "lectures" (he'd never say why: it's because nobody else always shows up for Bert) • Kati's improvement campaigns bounce off him entirely; he once said two words to her — "Schon gut." ("It's fine [as it is]") — and she has thought about it since.

**DO:** silence as dialogue, one-word completeness, football transformation, care through action.
**DON'T:** never chatty, never explains his feelings in words, never misses a match, never fails to return a Pfand bottle — and never, ever comment on his 90-minute emotional volcano afterwards.

## 5. CAST DYNAMICS (the comedy matrix)

|  | Rolf | Bert | Kati | Müller |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rolf** | — | annoyed audience / secret fondness | mutual unimpressed slow-burn | brothers in silence |
| **Bert** | one-man fan club | — | courtly admiration | unsolicited life coach |
| **Kati** | magnetic irritation | tolerated weather event | — | unimprovable project |
| **Müller** | the only one he "gets" | always shows up for him | "Schon gut." | — |

**The two silences vs. the two performers:** Rolf+Müller (quiet north/Berlin axis) against Bert+Kati (loud/polished performance axis) is the cast's structural comedy: pair across the axis for maximum friction, along it for warmth episodes.

**Belief collisions = episode generators:** Bert's "I understand everything" vs. reality • Kati's "perfection is achievable" vs. chaos (usually Bert) • Rolf's "caring is embarrassing" vs. moments that demand care • Müller's "words are expensive" vs. situations that demand words (ordering, phone calls, declarations — a goldmine for teaching functional German, since the LEARNER must supply what Müller won't say).

## 6. RULES FOR THE STORY PIPELINE (consistency contract)

1. **One belief challenged per episode.** Pick a character, poke their belief with a tiny everyday situation (a delayed train for Kati; a compliment for Rolf; a question Bert can't answer; a phone call Müller must make).
2. **The 10 target words get assigned to the character who OWNS that domain** (see per-character vocabulary domains) — the professor teaches food words wrongly-then-rightly, Müller teaches weather in one-word scenes, etc.
3. **Voice check before render:** if a line could be swapped between two characters unchanged, rewrite it. Rolf max 2 sentences; Müller max 2 words (except match day); Bert never under 2 exclamations; Kati never imprecise.
4. **Every episode ends on the human beat, not the joke:** one small unspoken moment of the wound or the warmth (Rolf almost caring, Bert being listened to, Kati un-smirking, Müller's silent gift). This is what builds the universe past the gag.
5. **Stereotype safety rail:** stereotypes are played as *loving self-aware archetypes* (the way Germans joke about themselves), never as mockery of real people. The characters are IN on it. Punch at beliefs, never at origins.
6. **Editability:** each character block is standalone. Version changes to beliefs/wounds get dated notes at the bottom of the block. The visual bible (sheets + master prompts) and this behavior bible together are the complete character definition.
