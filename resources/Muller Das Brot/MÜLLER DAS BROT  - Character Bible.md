# MÜLLER DAS BROT — Character Bible

**Region / Archetype:** the North — Hamburg direction, where people are famously reserved, not big talkers, cool as the North Sea wind, and warm only once you've earned it (but then: for life). The stare, the short answers, the beanie in all seasons. Football is his religion; Pfand collection is his economy. He goes by his surname — of course he does.

**Core belief:** *"Words are expensive. Spend them carefully."* Everything necessary can be said with a look, a nod, or a short clear sentence. If you must give a speech, something has already gone wrong.

**Inner depth (the wound):** he feels EVERYTHING and can express almost NOTHING. Generations of northern weather, wind, and history built people who ration emotion like coal in winter — the feelings are all there, fully formed, with no exit. His one release valve: football. During a match, Müller transforms for 90 minutes into a roaring, weeping, hugging volcano — then the final whistle blows and he is calm again, as if it never happened. Nobody mentions it. Everybody knows. His recurring tender moment: he expresses care through objects and actions — silently leaving your favorite drink at your door, standing wordlessly next to someone who is sad (which, from him, is a hug).

**Personality:**
- Short answers as complete communication: "Moin." covers hello, goodbye, agreement, congratulations, and condolences, depending on eyebrow position. When more is needed, he delivers it in brief, complete, clearly spoken sentences — and not one word more.
- The stare: his default interface with reality. Not hostile — processing. Unembarrassed observation.
- Thrift plus duty: the Pfand bag isn't shameful, it's CORRECT. A deposit system exists, therefore one uses it. Money AND environment. The system works because everyone participates; Müller participates.
- Reliable like tide tables. If Müller nodded that he'd come, he is already there.
- Cute against his will: the beanie, the smallness of his words against the bigness of his feelings. The audience adores him for exactly the things he'd hate to have noticed.

**Quirks:** counts his Pfand bottles like a dragon counts gold — his one visible joy outside football; trusts no forecast ("Bert said sun." *packs second jacket*); his crust zipper creaks when he's emotional and he pretends it didn't; keeps exactly one photo in his jacket, never fully shown.

**Voice & speaking style:** low, calm, slow, with a northern clip — words end early, as if conserving heat. Precisely because he says little, every word is delivered clearly and deliberately: the easiest German in the cast to learn from, one clean sentence at a time, framed by comfortable silence. Volume barely above quiet — except match day (full roar: "TOOOOR!").

**Signature phrases:** "Moin." • "Jo." / "Nee." • "Kalt." (his weather report, always accurate) • "Schon gut." • "TOOOOR!" (match day only).

**His vocabulary home turf (for stories):** greetings, weather (kalt, der Regen, der Wind), football (der Ball, das Tor, das Spiel, gewinnen), money-and-bottle words (die Flasche, das Geld, sparen).

**Relationships:**
- **Rolf:** soul brothers in silence. Their near-wordless scenes are their friendship.
- **Bert:** endures the unrequested life-coaching with the patience of a coastline enduring weather — and secretly attends every single "lecture." He would never say why. It's because nobody else always shows up for Bert.
- **Kati:** her improvement campaigns bounce off him entirely. His "Schon gut." is the only review of her worldview anyone has ever landed.

**Consistency notes:** silence as dialogue; care shown through action; the match-day transformation is never commented on afterwards by anyone; he never misses a match and never fails to return a bottle. When he speaks, he speaks slowly, clearly, completely — brevity never means mumbling.
