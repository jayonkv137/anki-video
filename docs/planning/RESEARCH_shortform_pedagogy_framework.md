## 0. One-paragraph thesis

Short-form vertical video (Reels/TikTok) is a viable delivery vehicle for **comprehensible input (CI)** if the long-form pedagogy of Dreaming Spanish, Nicos Weg, and Extr@ auf Deutsch is *re-engineered*, not merely shortened. In a 30–45s vertical clip the cultural stereotype must be the **scenography and the conflict** (visible in the first 3 seconds), while the spoken dialogue stays natural, high-density, and pedagogically structured. Four animated food archetypes dramatize 100 documented German micro-behaviors; the pipeline is tuned for cognitive load, visual processing, and CEFR-graded linguistic progression.

## 1. Format analysis — adapting long-form models to 9:16

| Long-form source | Core pedagogical mechanism | Primary visual scaffold | Compression / vertical adaptation (9:16) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dreaming Spanish** | Pure comprehensible input; zero-L1 translation; 7 input-level thresholds | Heavy physical gesturing, sketch-drawing, real-time illustration | Multi-layered character-sprite animation; expressive food archetypes denote concrete spatial relationships/actions instantly |
| **Nicos Weg** (DW) | Episodic situational drama mapping grammar → functional survival context | Realistic cinematic settings, linear plot, high-frequency natural dialogue | Hyper-condensed transactions around a single cultural micro-behavior (e.g. Mülltrennung), resolving in <45s |
| **Extr@ auf Deutsch** | Sitcom humor; hyper-enunciated speech; high visual-contextual predictability | Exaggerated expressions, physical comedy, repetitive TPRS structure | Kinetic typography, character close-ups in the central safe zone, high-contrast color-coding of lexical units |

**Key translation rules**
- Physical gesturing (Dreaming Spanish) → dynamic sprite animation; facial expression + kinetic typography carry meaning.
- Long contextual build-up (Nicos Weg) → immediate high-impact setup; the conflict (the stereotype) is on-screen in the first 3 seconds.
- Redundancy (Extr@) → maintained via tightly scripted micro-dialogues that repeat a target grammar pattern across rapid comedic turns (not via drawn-out scenes).

## 2. Empirical design parameters

### 2.1 Pacing & word limits across CEFR levels

Native German runs ~130–150 WPM (~5.0–6.0 syll/s). For A1–B1 this must be reduced. With σ = average syllable-to-word ratio ≈ **1.7** (German compounding):

```
WPM = (SR_sps / σ) × 60      where SR_sps = target syllables per second
```

| Level | SR (syll/s) | ≈ WPM | Video duration | Pausing budget | Script size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | ~2.25 | ~80 | 30 s | ~10 s silence/transitions | 25–30 words (3–4 sentences, ~7 words each) |
| **A2** | ~3.0 | ~100 | 40 s | ~8 s | 50–55 words (5–6 sentences, ~9 words each) |
| **B1** | ~4.0 | ~120 | 45 s | ~5 s | 75–80 words (7–8 sentences, ~10–11 words each) |

**Pauses are structural, not dead air.** Insert them at natural syntactic boundaries — before separable-verb prefixes, around subordinate clauses, at turn-taking switches. (Mirrors the Delayed-Auditory-Feedback slowing effect: prolongs vowels, stabilizes syllable transitions, lowers cognitive friction.)

### 2.2 Cognitive load (Mayer's CTML)

Learners process via independent, capacity-limited **visual** and **auditory** channels.

**The dual-subtitle trap:** showing L2 (German) + L1 (English) simultaneously creates a severe split-attention effect. Eye-tracking shows gaze collapses onto the L1 line (~70%), overriding facial cues and animation → shallow translation-based processing.

| Subtitle mode | Gaze allocation | Cognitive load | Vocab-form retention | Structural grammar acquisition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dual (L1 top + L2 bottom) | L1 70% / L2 25% / scene 5% | High | Moderate | **Low** |
| L2-only (verb-aligned) | L2 60% / scene 40% | Moderate | High | Moderate |
| **L2 + visual input enhancement (color key)** | **L2 80% / scene 20%** | Controlled | **Very high** | **Very high** |

### 2.3 Subtitle & highlight configuration (the winning mode)

- **Single-line German (L2) subtitles**, placed in close spatial proximity to the speaking character.
- **Word-by-word kinetic typography** — flash each word synchronized to speech to aid segmentation without clutter.
- **Grammatical-gender color-coding** of nouns (strong recall gains):

  | Gender | Article | Color | Hex |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Masculine | der | Blue | `#3B82F6` |
  | Feminine | die | Red | `#EF4444` |
  | Neuter | das | Green | `#10B981` |

- **Grammatical-case / target-feature highlighting** in **Yellow `#F59E0B`** — modal verbs, separable prefixes, adjective/case endings, target verbs. Draws the eye to structural change → "noticing" without explicit grammar instruction.

### 2.4 High-yield grammar for short form

Short clips are bad at overviews, ideal for high-yield context-dependent structures:
- **Modal verbs** (müssen, dürfen, sollen) — obligation/permission; visually demonstrable (physically block a character = *nicht dürfen*).
- **Separable verbs** (aufmachen, herstellen, wegschmeißen) — kinetic typography can slide the prefix to sentence-end to mirror word order.
- **Conversational particles** (doch, ja, mal, eben) — meaning conveyed through tone shifts, eye-rolls, cartoon SFX.

### 2.5 Visual integration of the stereotype (avoid "explaining the joke")

Do **not** use dialogue to explain the cultural habit. The stereotype is the **set and the dramatic conflict**; characters speak natural, practical German that resolves the immediate situation. Background posters, props, and body language carry the cultural context.

## 3. Content strategy blueprint

### 3.1 The four animated food archetypes

| Character | Register | Personality | Pedagogical role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rolf die Wurst** (rule-enforcing Bratwurst) | Formal / imperative; modal verbs of obligation | Hyper-orderly, literal, agitated by rule-breaking | **Enforcer** — authority & correction structures |
| **Kati die Kartoffel** (easy-going potato) | Informal, colloquial; shortened spoken forms (hab', 'n, 's) | Relaxed, confused by regulations | **Target** — of correction; prompts explanatory structures |
| **Bert das Bier** (social, particle-heavy Pilsner) | Slang-rich; high modal-particle density (doch, mal, halt) | Friendly, loud, over-prepared | **Catalyst** — drives interactive informal dialogue |
| **Müller das Brot** (melancholic sourdough) | Slow, weary; passive voice, Konjunktiv II | Anxious, over-cautious, worried about inefficiency | **Victim** — elicits empathy & polite forms |

> Note: our current canon cast/naming lives in `resources/Characters-Main-Sheet.md`. Reconcile role assignments against the bibles before locking (see vision doc open-questions).

### 3.2 Computational scripting matrix (hard constraints by level)

| Parameter | A1 | A2 | B1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Max duration | 30 s | 40 s | 45 s |
| Max word count | 30 | 55 | 80 |
| Max sentence length | 8 words | 12 words | 15 words |
| Core tenses | Präsens only | Präsens, Perfekt (haben/sein) | + Präteritum (aux), Futur I |
| Syntactic structures | SVO, basic W-questions | Coordinating conj. (und, aber, oder, denn) | Subordinating conj. (weil, dass, wenn, obwohl) |
| Target lexicon | Top 500 concrete | Top 1,000 situational | Top 2,000 abstract/idiomatic |

### 3.3 The five video typologies (every script must be one of these)

1. **Die Hausordnung (Rule Enforcement)** — modal verbs of permission/obligation + imperatives. Rolf catches Kati violating an ordinance; demands compliance. *(Rolf → Kati.)*
2. **Das Missverständnis (Cultural Misunderstanding)** — Perfekt narration + clarification structures. Kati misperforms a habit; Müller explains the protocol. *(Müller → Kati.)*
3. **Allzeit Bereit (Extreme Preparedness)** — conditional (wenn…dann) + future intent (werden + inf.). Bert invites Müller on a short walk; Müller arrives in full expedition gear. *(Bert ↔ Müller.)*
4. **Der Pfand-Krieg (Efficiency Crisis)** — comparatives/superlatives + causal connectors (weil, deshalb). A dented bottle jams the Pfand machine; high-stakes debate over 25 cents. *(Bert ↔ Rolf.)*
5. **Der stumme Vorwurf (Silent Accusation)** — Konjunktiv II for polite frustration + indirect questions. Müller skipped his Kehrwoche; Rolf sweeps aggressively at his feet, clearing his throat. *(Rolf → Müller.)*

## 4. Worked example — "Stoßlüften & Querlüften" (A2, typology 1)

**Objective:** imperative verbs, separable prefixes (aufmachen/zumachen), modal müssen. **Stereotype:** shock-ventilation ritual. **Cast:** Rolf (ventilation expert) + Kati (freezing companion); Bert & Müller as passive room elements.

**9:16 safe-zone architecture (1080×1920):**
- Top UI zone 0–270 px — keep text out (time, profile, progress bar).
- Active visual safe zone 270–1250 px — 65 px left/right margin guards; characters + action; **subtitle Y-anchor 1150 px** (under characters' feet).
- Bottom UI zone 1250–1920 px — keep text out (like/comment/share/caption).

**Script (42 words, ~84 WPM, 5 pauses):**

| Seg | Time | Line (color-coded) | Pedagogical purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–0:10 | "Es riecht stickig! **Die Luft**(red) ist verbraucht!" | descriptive adjectives; feminine noun *die Luft* |
| 2 | 0:10–0:22 | "**Mach**(yellow) sofort **das Fenster**(green) **auf**(yellow), Kati! Wir **müssen**(yellow) **stoßlüften**(yellow)!" | separable imperative (*mach auf!*); modal → sentence-final infinitive (*müssen stoßlüften*) |
| 3 | 0:22–0:35 | Kati: "Es ist eiskalt! Ich **friere**(yellow)!" · Rolf: "Nein, das ist gesund! Nur noch fünf Minuten!" | natural reaction phrases; high-freq adjectives |
| 4 | 0:35–0:45 | "Perfekt! **Der Sauerstoff**(blue) ist wieder da!" | masculine noun declension; comedic resolution |

Visual beats: stale-air yellow mist → Rolf lunges to windows → cartoon blizzard freezes Bert (ice block) & Müller → windows slam, green stamp "Luftqualität: PERFEKT!". Subtitle box: bold sans, ~64 pt, rounded black background.

## 5. Integration standards for the pipeline (the checklist)

1. **Syntactic pacing control** — enforce syllable-per-second ceilings per CEFR tier; insert pauses at clause boundaries.
2. **Visual interface compliance** — lock all kinetic typography + key interactions inside the central safe zone.
3. **Consistent visual input enhancement** — auto-apply gender color-coding (blue/red/green) to nouns + yellow to target grammar features.
4. **Stereotype as scenography** — never explain the habit in dialogue; encode it in set/props/body language.
5. **One typology per script** — pick from the five; it fixes the grammar target and the character pairing.
