# Deep-Research Prompt — Human-in-the-Loop AI Co-Creation System for the Ideation Stage

> **Purpose:** paste the block below into a deep-research tool (Gemini Deep Research, etc.) to research how to build the *creative-development / brainstorming* stage of the "Stereotypical German" pipeline — the co-creation that happens **before** scripting. Not about video models or pedagogy (those are researched — see `RESEARCH_v3_tech_derisk` + `RESEARCH_shortform_pedagogy_framework`). This is about the **human + AI story-development method** and its prior art.

## THE PROMPT (copy from here) ⬇

You are a senior research analyst specializing in **computational creativity, human–AI co-creativity, and instructional/narrative design**. Produce a comprehensive, citation-backed report that will let me design ONE specific part of a content pipeline.

**What I'm building (context):** a daily production system for short-form (30–45s, 9:16 vertical) German-language-learning videos for Instagram. A fixed cast of **four recurring characters** (each with a defined personality, speech register, and pedagogical role) lives in one consistent, photorealistic world. Each episode dramatizes **one German cultural stereotype** (from a library of ~100) and teaches **one language point** through natural, relatable dialogue — the stereotype is the *setting and the conflict*, never explained in the dialogue.

**The exact problem to research:** I need to design the **creative-development stage that happens BEFORE any script is written** — a **repeatable, human-in-the-loop, AI-assisted co-creation process**. Each day, the human (me) and an LLM collaborate, turn by turn, to develop a chosen stereotype + chosen characters into an original, relatable, pedagogically sound, entertaining **story concept**, built **block by block**:
`chosen stereotype → choose characters (1 mandatory main + optional side/guest/background) → choose setting/location → develop premise & conflict → choose the language lesson it will teach → choose the comedic/creative angle → commit a one-line logline + structured brief.`
At each block the AI should propose **3–5 genuinely distinct options** with reasoning, invite my own ideas, and converge with me. The end state is a crisp brief that feeds a downstream screenplay → storyboard → video pipeline.

**My hard requirements:** (1) I must retain **real creative authorship** every day — this must fuse *my* taste and seed ideas with AI generation, NOT produce generic "AI slop" (anti-slop is a brand pillar). (2) It must be a **replicable "skill"** (a structured LLM workflow + prompt patterns + decision gates) that nonetheless yields a **unique, strong** concept each run. (3) Tone is **realistic and relatable**, not random slapstick. (4) It must scale to **~100 episodes** with a fixed cast/world that still feels fresh.

**Research these questions in depth, each as its own section:**

1. **Prior art — tools & products.** Survey systems for AI-assisted collaborative story/scenario/screenplay development and pre-viz: e.g. DeepMind **Dramatron**, **Sudowrite** (Story Engine/Muse), **NovelAI**, **LTX Studio**, **Lore Machine**, Fable Studio **Showrunner/SHOW-1**, **Character.AI**, **Subtxt/Dramatica**, **Plotdrive**, **Squibler**, plus any language-teaching-specific content generators. For each: how they structure human–AI turn-taking, what creative control the human keeps, how they hold character/world consistency, and documented strengths/failure modes. **Give a comparison table.**
2. **Academic frameworks — mixed-initiative & co-creative AI.** Synthesize the core research on **mixed-initiative co-creativity (MICI)**, **creativity-support tools (CSTs)**, and human–AI co-writing (e.g. Riedl, Guzdial, Kreminski, Compton's "casual creators", Deterding). Which design principles reliably increase the human's **agency, ownership, and satisfaction** while raising output quality? How do effective systems separate **divergent** (option-generating) from **convergent** (deciding) phases?
3. **Anti-homogenization / anti-"AI slop."** Why does LLM ideation collapse toward generic sameness, and what are the **proven countermeasures** (sampling controls, persona/constraint conditioning, forced diversity along explicit axes, human-seed injection, retrieval of specific references, self-critique/critic models, combinatorial/morphological generation)? How do I guarantee 100 episodes feel distinct?
4. **Structured ideation methods mapped to LLM prompting.** Map classic techniques (SCAMPER, morphological analysis, "What if…" laddering, Osborn–Parnes divergent→convergent, Disney method, Six Thinking Hats, TRIZ) onto **concrete prompt patterns** for my block-by-block flow. For each block (character dynamic, location, premise/conflict, language lesson, creative/comedic angle, logline) give a recommended prompting strategy and how to present 3–5 distinct options.
5. **Micro-story architecture for short-form comedy.** Which narrative unit best fits a 30–45s single-conflict comedic language clip — **scene, sketch, vignette, sequence, montage, or single-joke cold-open**? Synthesize sketch-comedy and advertising craft (premise, the UCB "game of the scene," escalation, button/tag) and show how a **cultural stereotype becomes the "game"/central conflict** without being explained in dialogue.
6. **Pedagogy-into-story integration.** How to **co-select the teaching point** (grammar target, high-frequency vocab, modal particles) so it emerges *naturally* from the scenario rather than being bolted on — grounded in comprehensible input (Krashen), **TPRS "story-asking,"** and task-based language teaching, at A1–B1. How should the system present a menu of "what could this stereotype teach?" options for me to choose?
7. **Human-in-the-loop workflow & UX patterns.** Best practices for **conversational, gated/wizard co-creation interfaces**: when to use guided step-by-step vs open chat; how to present options, capture my freeform ideas mid-flow, remember decisions (state/memory), allow backtracking, and end on a committed concept. Reference how leading creative tools do this.
8. **Replicability & a variety engine.** How to make this a deterministic-enough **skill** that still yields novelty: the role of a tracked **library + coverage log** (my 100 stereotypes, what's been used), **typology rotation**, and **controlled randomness** (e.g., surfacing 3 random unused stereotypes daily). How do serialized franchises keep a fixed cast + world feeling fresh over many episodes?

**Deliverable format:** (a) executive summary of the recommended co-creation system; (b) prior-art comparison table; (c) a concrete **step-by-step workflow for my block-by-block flow**, with the exact LLM prompt pattern for each step and how options are generated & presented; (d) a catalog of **anti-slop + variety techniques** with when to use each; (e) a short annotated bibliography of the most useful papers/tools; (f) pitfalls and failure modes to design against. **Prioritize specific, implementable guidance with citations over generic overviews.**
