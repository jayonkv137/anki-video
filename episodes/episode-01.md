# EPISODE 01 — "Der Vogel" (The Bird)

**Purpose:** first manual production trial (Flow, Jayon-driven). Also the GOLD-STANDARD reference episode — when C2 automates story+screenplay, its output must look like this document.
**Words (deck positions 1–10):** der Schauspieler (actor) · das Adjektiv (adjective) · der Erwachsene (adult) · der Nachmittag (afternoon) · die Luft (air) · der Flughafen (airport) · am Leben (alive) · das Tier (animal) · die Wohnung (apartment) · der Apfel (apple)
**Main cast (2):** **Bert das Bier** (belief challenged: *"I understand everything"*) + **Müller das Brot** (the silent counterweight — the loud/quiet friction axis). **Cameos:** Kati (scene 7), Rolf (scene 9).
**Episode engine (per bible):** Bert's belief meets a tiny situation his theories can't solve; Müller solves it without a word; the human beat closes it.

**LOGLINE:** On a quiet afternoon, a pigeon lies motionless in front of the apartment. Bert produces theory after wrong theory to save it; Müller silently does the one thing that works. The bird was fine all along — and gets a home.

---

## SCREENPLAY (10 scenes · one word each · ~6–8s per scene · dialogue lines ≤5s)

### Scene 1 — der Nachmittag *(afternoon)*
**Setting:** warm afternoon light, bench in front of their apartment building; Bert mid-gesture, Müller motionless with Pfand bag.
**Action:** Establishing shot. Bert stretches contentedly, gestures at the sun. Müller stares straight ahead.
**Dialogue:**
- BERT: „Ah! Der Nachmittag ist schön! Prost, Nachmittag!" *(raises mini-mug)*
- MÜLLER: „Jo."

### Scene 2 — das Tier *(animal)*
**Setting:** same bench; a plump pigeon lies on the pavement in front of them, very still, one wing splayed theatrically.
**Action:** Both look down slowly. The pigeon does not move. Müller's stare intensifies by one eyebrow.
**Dialogue:**
- BERT: „Müller! Ein Tier! Da liegt ein Tier!"
- MÜLLER: *(stares)* „Moin."

### Scene 3 — am Leben *(alive)*
**Setting:** close on the pigeon; Bert's face lowers into frame, upside-down, glasses slipping.
**Action:** Bert inspects at zero distance. The pigeon's eye opens one millimeter, then closes.
**Dialogue:**
- BERT: „Ist der Vogel am Leben?! Vogel! Bist du am Leben?"
- *(pigeon: absolute silence)*

### Scene 4 — die Luft *(air)*
**Setting:** Bert fanning the pigeon frantically with his Tyrolean hat; foam hair inflated to maximum.
**Action:** THEORY #1. Bert fans hard. A leaf blows away. The pigeon does not move.
**Dialogue:**
- BERT: „Ich habe eine Idee! Er braucht Luft! Ganz einfach!"
- BERT: *(fanning)* „Luft! Luft! Luft!"

### Scene 5 — der Apfel *(apple)*
**Setting:** Müller silently holds out a small piece of apple toward the pigeon, arm fully extended, face unchanged.
**Action:** THEORY #2 arrives without words. The pigeon ignores the apple. Müller retracts arm 2 cm. Waits.
**Dialogue:**
- BERT: „Ja! Essen! Ein Apfel! Äpfel sind gut!"
- MÜLLER: *(offering the apple)* „…"

### Scene 6 — das Adjektiv *(adjective)*
**Setting:** Bert in full professor pose, one glass arm raised, lecturing the motionless pigeon.
**Action:** THEORY #3, now purely academic. Müller checks his Pfand bag in the background.
**Dialogue:**
- BERT: „Der Vogel ist müde. Müde ist ein Adjektiv! Ich weiß alles!"

### Scene 7 — der Schauspieler *(actor)* — KATI CAMEO
**Setting:** Kati walks through frame, immaculate, barely slowing down; she glances at the pigeon for exactly one second.
**Action:** She delivers the diagnosis over her shoulder and keeps walking. The pigeon's eye opens slightly — caught.
**Dialogue:**
- KATI: „Natürlich. Der Vogel ist ein Schauspieler."
- BERT: „Ein… Schauspieler?!"

### Scene 8 — der Flughafen *(airport)*
**Setting:** a plane passes high above; the pigeon's head snaps up, fully alive, watching it with longing.
**Action:** The "dying" pigeon stands, stretches both wings, stares at the sky like a commuter at a departure board.
**Dialogue:**
- BERT: „Das Flugzeug fliegt zum Flughafen! Und der Vogel… wartet?!"
- MÜLLER: „Jo."

### Scene 9 — der Erwachsene *(adult)* — ROLF CAMEO
**Setting:** Rolf leans in doorway behind them, deadpan, watching Bert who is now weeping with joy.
**Action:** Bert dabs his eyes with his moustache foam. Rolf observes. Pigeon preens.
**Dialogue:**
- BERT: *(crying, happy)* „Er ist am Leben! ER IST AM LEBEN!"
- ROLF: „Erwachsene weinen nicht." *(beat)* „…Egal."

### Scene 10 — die Wohnung *(apartment)* — RESOLUTION + HUMAN BEAT
**Setting:** the pigeon flutters up to a small nest above their window. Evening light. Bert and Müller look up from the bench.
**Action:** The pigeon settles into the nest. Müller — silently, without any announcement — places a piece of apple on the windowsill below the nest. Bert notices, says nothing for once. **The human beat:** care shown through objects, Müller's signature.
**Dialogue:**
- BERT: *(soft, for him)* „Der Vogel hat eine Wohnung. Hier. Bei uns."
- MÜLLER: *(to the pigeon, a nod)* „Moin."

## Voice check (bible rule 3) ✅
Bert: never under 2 exclamations, wrong-but-committed theories, toast, cries at scene 9. Müller: 6 words total across the episode ("Jo"×2, "Moin"×2, silence) — care through the apple, not words. Kati: one precise, devastating line. Rolf: dry, two sentences, ends on „Egal."

## Word placement check ✅
Every scene genuinely uses its word (bold in dialogue/action); Adjektiv handled via dialogue (escape hatch); am Leben used 3× naturally (scenes 3, 9). All A1/A2, sentences ≤ 8 words, present tense.

## Production notes for the Flow trial
- Attach **Bert + Müller** as characters everywhere; Kati only scene 7, Rolf only scene 9. The pigeon is a PROP-level extra (plain grey pigeon, slightly round, no character sheet — allowed to vary).
- Per scene prompt = [your style block] + Setting + Action (above) + dialogue via colon-trick: `Bert says: Ist der Vogel am Leben?`
- Keep spoken lines ≤5s; scenes ~6–8s; 9:16; ×1 samples; Confirm=Always; cheapest model first (Omni Flash / Veo Fast), Quality only for keepers.
- **Log per scene:** credits spent · retakes · what drifted (character? style? pigeon?). That log = our first real C3 data.
- Continuity anchors: bench + building door recur in scenes 1, 2, 9, 10 — generate scene 1 first and reuse its look as reference where Flow allows.
